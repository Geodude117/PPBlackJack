Soft Hands in Blackjack Explained

A soft hand is any hand with an Ace counted as 11. They’re the hands most players get wrong, and once you see why, they’re some of the easiest to play well.

What makes a hand soft?

A hand is soft when it holds an Ace that’s counted as 11. A,6 is soft 17: 11 + 6. Because that Ace can drop to 1 whenever you need it to, a soft hand can’t bust on the next card.

A hand with no Ace, or with every Ace counted as 1, is hard. Hit A,6 and draw a 9, and the Ace has to become 1: the hand is now hard 16.

In Perfect Pair Blackjack the total shows “soft” while the Ace is still counting as 11, for example “soft 17”. Two-card 21 shows as blackjack instead.

How often do you get a soft hand?

With six decks, about 1 in 10 starting hands is soft (10.1%): an Ace with anything from 2 to 9, or a pair of Aces. An Ace with a 10-value card is blackjack, which comes up a further 4.7% of the time.

Why hitting soft 17 is safe

Many players stand on soft 17 because “17 is a good total”. It isn’t: a dealer who stands on 17 only wins against 16 or lower. Hitting can’t bust you, and here’s roughly what the next card does:

5 to 9Hard 12–16 (can hit again)38%
10, J, Q, KHard 17 (no worse)31%

So basic strategy never stands on soft 17 or lower. Against a dealer’s 3 to 6, it doubles instead.

Soft hand strategy in one minute

For 6 decks, dealer hits soft 17, double after split allowed (the rules in this game):

  • Soft 13 and 14 (A,2 and A,3): double against 5 or 6, otherwise hit.
  • Soft 15 and 16 (A,4 and A,5): double against 4, 5 or 6, otherwise hit.
  • Soft 17 (A,6): double against 3 to 6, otherwise hit.
  • Soft 18 (A,7): double against 2 to 6, stand against 7 or 8, hit against 9, 10 or Ace.
  • Soft 19 (A,8): stand, except double against a 6.
  • Soft 20 (A,9): always stand.

If you can’t double (you’ve already taken a card, or can’t cover the bet), hit, except on soft 18 and 19, where you stand. The full grid is on the basic strategy chart.

When the dealer hits soft 17

Tables are labelled either “dealer stands on soft 17” (S17) or “dealer hits soft 17” (H17). Perfect Pair Blackjack uses H17, like most UK and US casino tables. Here’s how the dealer’s final hand works out under each rule, worked out exactly for a full 6-deck shoe across every up card:

Hitting soft 17 turns some dealer 17s into 18 to 21, and busts a little more often. Overall it’s worse for the player: it’s generally put at about 0.2% added to the house edge, which is why S17 tables are worth looking for in a real casino.

FAQ

Is A,6 a soft 17?

Yes. The Ace counts as 11, so A,6 is soft 17. If you draw a card that would take it over 21, the Ace drops to 1 and the hand becomes hard.

Can you bust a soft hand?

Not with one card. The Ace can always drop from 11 to 1, so the worst one hit can do is turn it into a hard total. After that the hand can bust like any other.

Should you stand on soft 18?

Against a dealer’s 7 or 8, yes. Against 2 to 6, double if you can. Against 9, 10 or Ace, hit: soft 18 loses too often to those cards.

Does the dealer hit soft 17 in Perfect Pair Blackjack?

Yes. The dealer hits soft 17 and stands on hard 17 and above, and the strategy hints in the game are built for that rule.
