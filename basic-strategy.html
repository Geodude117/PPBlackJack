How to read the chart

Work out which table your hand belongs in, in this order:

  1. Pairs: two cards of the same value, such as 8,8 or A,A. Use this table first.
  2. Soft hands: hands with an Ace counted as 11, such as A,6 (soft 17).
  3. Hard hands: everything else, including any hand where the Ace has to count as 1.

The rules worth remembering

  • Always split Aces and 8s. Never split 10s or 5s.
  • Stand on hard 17 or more. Always hit hard 8 or less.
  • Dealer showing 2 to 6? Stand on hard 13 to 16. Those up cards bust most often, so let the dealer take the risk. (Hit 12 against a 2 or 3.)
  • Dealer showing 7 or higher? Hit until you reach hard 17.
  • Double on 11 against anything, on 10 against 2 to 9, and on 9 against 3 to 6.
  • Soft 18 is a hit against 9, 10 or Ace. It feels wrong, but it’s right.
  • Never take insurance. Insurance pays 2:1 but the dealer has blackjack less than a third of the time, so it loses money in the long run, even when you hold a blackjack.

Basic strategy FAQ

What is blackjack basic strategy?

Basic strategy is the mathematically best way to play every blackjack hand, based only on your cards and the dealer’s up card. It doesn’t guarantee you win a hand, but it gives the lowest house edge over time.

Should you always split 8s and Aces?

Yes. With these rules you should always split a pair of Aces and a pair of 8s, whatever the dealer shows. A pair of 8s is 16, the worst total, and two Aces give two strong starting hands.

Should you ever split 10s?

No. A pair of 10s is 20, one of the best hands in the game, so basic strategy says always stand.

When should you double down on 11?

With six decks and a dealer who hits soft 17, double down on a hard 11 against every dealer up card, including an Ace.

Does this chart work for any blackjack game?

It’s built for 6 decks, dealer hits soft 17, double on any two cards, double after split allowed, and no surrender, which are the rules in Perfect Pair Blackjack. Other rule sets change a handful of borderline decisions.
