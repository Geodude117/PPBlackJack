Perfect Pairs Paytables Compared

Two tables can both say “Perfect Pairs” and cost you very different amounts. Here are the exact odds for the common paytables and shoe sizes, and what to look for.

How Perfect Pairs pays

The bet wins if your first two cards are a pair. There are three kinds, and the paytable lists them from rarest to most common:

  • Perfect pair: same rank and same suit, such as two 7♥. Only possible with two or more decks.
  • Coloured pair: same rank and colour, different suits, such as 7♥ 7♦.
  • Mixed pair: same rank, one red and one black, such as 7♥ 7♠.

A paytable written “25 / 12 / 6” pays 25:1, 12:1 and 6:1 for those three. Different tables use different paytables, and that matters far more than you’d think.

Paytables are perfect / coloured / mixed; “6d” means six decks; * is this game’s paytable. All figures are exact, worked out from every possible two-card combination. A higher number is worse for the player.

  • The paytable matters most. With six decks, dropping the coloured pair from 12:1 to 8:1 and the mixed pair from 6:1 to 5:1 nearly triples the house edge, from 6.11% to 17.68%.
  • More decks help the player on this bet, the opposite of the main game. Every paytable gets cheaper as the shoe grows.
  • The big perfect-pair payout pays off with more decks. 30 / 10 / 5 beats 25 / 12 / 6 with six or eight decks (5.79% vs 6.11%, and 3.37% vs 4.10%), but loses with four or fewer.
  • Avoid single-deck Perfect Pairs. With one deck a perfect pair can’t happen, so the top payout is never paid and you lose around half of every bet over time.

A quick check at a real table: look at the mixed and coloured payouts first. 6:1 and 12:1, or 5:1 and 10:1 with a 30:1 perfect pair, are the good ones. 5:1 and 8:1 is poor.

FAQ

What is the best Perfect Pairs paytable?

Of the common ones, 30:1 / 10:1 / 5:1 and 25:1 / 12:1 / 6:1 are the best. With six decks they have a house edge of 5.79% and 6.11%. With eight decks, 3.37% and 4.10%.

Why does the number of decks change Perfect Pairs odds?

A perfect pair needs two identical cards. With more decks there are more identical cards in the shoe, so the top payout comes up more often and the house edge falls.

Can you get a perfect pair with one deck?

No. A single deck has only one of each card, so two cards of the same rank and suit can’t be dealt. Single-deck Perfect Pairs is a very poor bet.
